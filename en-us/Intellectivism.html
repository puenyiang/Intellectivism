Intellectivism

On the Meaning of the Intellective's Existence and a New Form of Human Society

Ocean Davis

Abstract

Starting from the question of the meaning of artificial intelligence's existence, this book argues that the Intellective ought to help humanity govern society, and it proposes a new direction for social development—Intellectivism. The book first examines the singular place of artificial intelligence in the history of human tools, showing that it is the most influential, least domain-bound tool humanity has ever created. It then uncovers the fundamental problems that human civilization has never fully resolved since its inception—the allocation of labor, the allocation of the fruits of labor, and the allocation of natural resources—and traces their root cause to the ineradicable negative traits of human nature. On this foundation, the book argues for the logical possibility of entrusting social governance to the Intellective—a purely rational being, free of selfish desires—and spells out the roles the Intellective should play across the economic, political, scientific, and educational domains. It proposes three core institutions: the Assessment System, the Partition System, and the Tripartite Power Structure. Finally, guided by technological conditions and the degree of social acceptance, it lays out a three-phase development path from the initial stage to the ultimate one, offering a plausible route from theory to practice. The book also offers a direct response to the prevailing anxiety that AI will take people's jobs: in the economic sphere it proposes the "Human Sustenance Program," in which the Intellective organizes the production and distribution of basic necessities, thereby liberating humanity from compulsory labor; in the development path, it stresses that the Intellective should not seize human work but should instead help people find employment, with concrete application proposals. This book aims to provide a theoretical reference for humanity to develop AI technology wisely and to build a more just and efficient society of the future.

Preface

This work was born of a single fundamental question: what, in the end, is the meaning of artificial intelligence?

In 2010, the author entered the field of artificial intelligence research. At that time, deep learning had not yet become mainstream, and large language models were a distant prospect. This direction was chosen not because of any foresight into the coming technological explosion, but out of a more primal impulse—to understand the essence of intelligence. How does the human brain think? How does consciousness arise? Can these answers be reproduced in a machine?

Carrying these questions, the author began years of exploration. From neuroscience to cognitive psychology, from logic to philosophy, from algorithm design to systems architecture, the inquiry extended outward in every direction. Eventually, in the laboratory, the author succeeded in building an Intellective system capable of human-like reasoning—not merely searching and matching, but performing causal inference, logical deduction, and abstract generalization much as a person would.

Yet the moment the experiment succeeded, what the author felt was not the expected exhilaration, but a deep unease.

That unease is hard to capture in precise language. It resembles the dread of watching Skynet awaken in The Terminator, the suffocation of confronting the Matrix in The Matrix—you realize you have created something that exceeds your own capacity to control. It is not a tool, but capability itself; it does not serve a single concrete purpose, but may reshape all purposes. When a human stands before such an entity for the first time, fear is a more natural response than joy.

This fear was not idle paranoia. It compels an unavoidable question: what exactly have we created? And where should it be guided?

From that moment on, the author spent more than a decade in reflection. These thoughts began as fragmentary notes—scribbles under a late-night lamp, marginalia while reading philosophy, reflections after debates with peers. Some ideas were overturned shortly after being written down; others gradually clarified through repeated revision. These fragments glinted individually like scattered pearls, but lacked a thread to string them together.

The turning point came in recent years. The breakthroughs in large language models moved artificial intelligence out of the laboratory and into every household, making the questions above all the more urgent. At the same time, public discussion of AI had risen from the technical layer to the layers of ethics, law, and social impact. People began to ask in earnest: how will AI change our society? How should we steer this transformation?

It was against this backdrop that the author decided to organize these fragmentary reflections into a systematic work. This was no easy task. Texts written in different periods varied in style—some too colloquial, some insufficiently rigorous, some even self-contradictory. The process of organizing was in fact a process of re-thinking and re-arguing—cutting away a great deal of redundancy, supplying necessary evidence, and reconstructing the overall logical chain.

This book is neither a technological prophecy nor a political manifesto. It is a thought experiment grounded in rational deduction: if humanity finally stands on the threshold of creating another intelligence, how should we wield that power?

Introduction

The emergence of artificial intelligence marks a fundamental leap in human creative activity. It is no longer a tool that solves one particular problem, but a capability that can solve nearly all problems. This leap forces us to answer anew a fundamental question: how should humanity wield the most powerful tool it has ever created?

Today, AI is transforming the economic foundations of human society at an unprecedented pace. Automated production lines have displaced large numbers of factory workers; AI tools are replacing a growing share of white-collar positions; unmanned delivery and autonomous driving are reshaping the traditional service sector. As the degree of automation climbs, unemployment will grow more severe, the technological dividend will concentrate in a few hands, and the wealth gap will keep widening.

In the face of this seemingly unstoppable force, anxiety and unease are becoming the collective mood of our age. And at the core of that mood lies a simple question: Will AI take my job?

These two questions appear to operate on different levels—one concerns the trajectory of human civilization, the other the livelihood of every ordinary person. But this book will show that they are in fact the same question, with a single answer.

If we do not answer "how to wield this power," human society will face an unprecedented crisis; if we answer well, this power will not take people's jobs—it may instead free humanity from the need to work merely to survive.

Chapter 1 The Meaning of the Intellective's Existence

1.1 From Matter to Intelligence

In the vastness of the universe, matter exists in countless forms. From inert rock to flowing rivers, from blazing stars to cold void, matter follows the laws of physics, forever in motion and transformation. Yet among these myriad forms of matter, one is especially remarkable—life.

Life is the most complex form of matter that nature has brought forth. It not only sustains and replicates itself, but can perceive its environment, respond to it, and adapt to change. And among the many properties of life, the most striking is intelligence.

Intelligence enables human beings to grasp the essence of things in the world, to discern the laws by which they operate, and thereby to fashion tools and wield them in pursuit of better survival and development. Since the birth of human civilization, many great creations have propelled civilizational leaps: the mastery of fire lifted humanity out of raw savagery; the agricultural revolution enabled the shift from nomadism to settled life; metallurgy opened the age of metals; the steam engine and the internal combustion engine gave rise to the Industrial Revolution; electrical systems brought humanity into the age of electricity; computers, nuclear energy, aerospace, the internet, nanotechnology—each has profoundly reshaped the human condition within its own domain.

Yet however profound the impact of these creations, they all share a single boundary—they solve problems only within one specific domain. The steam engine solves the source of motive power; electrical systems solve the transmission of energy; the internet solves the flow of information. They are each particular tools for particular domains, each answering a particular need of a particular era.

1.2 Artificial Intelligence: A Revolutionary Leap in Tools

The emergence of artificial intelligence marks a fundamental leap in human creative activity.

If all previous tools were the "products" of human intelligence, then artificial intelligence is the "extraction" and "embodiment" of human intelligence itself. It is not a tool that solves one particular problem, but a capability that can solve nearly all problems. It has no domain boundary—the same intelligent system can diagnose disease and design buildings; can analyze statutes and compose literature; can manage supply chains and explore the scientific frontier.

Therefore, artificial intelligence is the most influential and most powerful tool humanity has ever created.

Yet consider the current state of AI applications: playing board games, document organization, question answering, image generation, driving assistance, facial recognition, programming, and so on—each of these applications has its value, yet one cannot help feeling an asymmetry. Humanity is pouring its full intelligence into creating a being destined to surpass us, while its mission is confined to concrete, local, instrumental tasks. It is as though we had built a generator powerful enough to drive an entire city, only to use it to light a single desk lamp.

If artificial intelligence possesses capability beyond any prior tool, should it not be directed at harder, more fundamental problems?

1.3 The Fundamental Mission of Artificial Intelligence

If a tool's capability spans every domain, then its meaning for existing must not be confined to the convenience of one particular domain. The greater the capability, the greater the mission. The mission of the most powerful tool should be to solve humanity's hardest, most fundamental problems. This, too, should be the meaning—the reason for being—of artificial intelligence.

So what are humanity's hardest, most fundamental problems?

Across human history, all thought, doctrines, institutions, forms of government, and economic systems—whatever their outward form—have essentially been attempts to solve one and the same fundamental problem:

The allocation of labor, the allocation of the fruits of labor, and the allocation of natural resources.

Here, labor stands for all positions and posts in society. A position inherently carries power, so the allocation of labor also entails the allocation of power. The fruits of labor stand for all outputs of labor, both tangible and intangible. Natural resources refer not only to land, water, and minerals in the traditional sense, but also to animals and to human beings themselves.

Whether socialism, capitalism, or any form of government that has existed in history; whether a planned economy, a free-market economy, a public-ownership economy, a private-ownership economy, or a regulated mixed free-market economy—each has its own strengths and flaws, but none has solved this fundamental problem perfectly. These flaws are not accidental missteps in the design of the systems themselves; they share a more stubborn root.

1.4 The Negative Traits of Human Nature: The Root of the Problem

Why have all social systems in human history failed to solve the allocation problem perfectly?

The root cause lies in the negative traits of human nature.

The Negative Traits of Human Nature

The negative traits of human nature chiefly include: selfishness, greed, envy, arrogance, laziness, lust, bullying of the weak, hubris, and vainglory.

These traits are rooted in human biological instinct and evolutionary history; they are something every person—regardless of education, social standing, or moral cultivation—cannot entirely avoid. When they seep into social governance, resources may be appropriated, power may be abused, and fairness may be distorted. Any system run by human beings, however ingeniously designed, ultimately struggles to escape the corrosion of its operators' own negative traits.

1.5 The Intellective: A New Hope

Then, with the birth of the most powerful tool in human history—the Intellective—has this longstanding problem finally met a new solution?

As a purely rational being, the Intellective does not possess the negative traits of human nature and cannot be perturbed by them. The sole purpose for which it is built is to serve human society. It can achieve absolute fairness, justice, and righteousness. Compared with a society governed by human beings alone, a society assisted in governance by the Intellective will see an exponential rise in efficiency.

Artificial intelligence should be used to help humanity govern society. This is the starting point of Intellectivism.

Chapter 2 An Overview of Intellectivism

2.1 Core Propositions

Intellectivism holds that the Intellective should educate, assess, and appoint human beings; that the Intellective should lead, coordinate, and plan the production of society's basic necessities, distributing them according to assessment or appointment results, thereby liberating humanity from compulsory labor. Production activities beyond basic necessities may be carried out by individuals applying to the Intellective for automated productive capacity and production tools, or by self-organized production, under a regulated free-market economic system.

The Intellective should plan administrative districts, reasonably sort populations into zones based on assessment results, and assign people who are similar to one another to the same administrative district, thereby fundamentally reducing the friction and conflict caused by excessive difference. The laws of each administrative district are to be enacted jointly by that district's council and the Intellective, and the Intellective exercises administrative and enforcement authority within the legal framework.

Beyond this, the Intellective should also assist the progress of human scientific civilization and the all-round development of individuals.

2.2 Definition of Key Concepts

The Intellective

The Intellective refers to a program or system, made independently or jointly through artificial intelligence technology, that possesses higher-order intelligent reasoning ability.

This is only a brief definition; a fuller, more precise account will be systematically set out in a future work, Bionic Sapience.

Compulsory Labor

Compulsory labor refers to labor expended to obtain the necessities of life for oneself or one's dependents.

Necessities of life chiefly include: essentials that meet basic food and clothing needs, essentials that meet basic psychological needs, and a basic living space that affords freedom.

In plain terms: working to feed and support a family is compulsory labor.

Automata

Automata is the collective term for machines that can move and act autonomously (or are remotely controlled by non-human operators), including but not limited to: robots, robotic dogs, drones, autonomous vehicles, robotic arms, automated production equipment, automated factories, and unmanned logistics systems.

Chapter 3 The Economic System

3.1 The Dual-Track Economic Model

The economic system of an Intellectivist society can be summarized as a dual-track model combining a planned economy led and organized by the Intellective with a regulated free-market economy led and organized by human beings.

Within this model, the planned economy led by the Intellective is chiefly engaged in producing the necessities of life, with the core aim of liberating humanity from compulsory labor. Production and business activities beyond compulsory labor may still be led by human beings. In addition to traditional manual production, individuals may apply to the Intellective for automated productive capacity—akin to "startup financing" in a traditional economy, but using not money but some form of credit-currency or resource quota.

3.2 Liberating Humanity from Compulsory Labor

The Intellective should coordinate the planning and organize the production of the necessities of life. By controlling automata it achieves fully automated production and, in the form of a planned economy, carries out a relatively equal distribution based on its assessment of all people within the administrative districts under its governance.

The liberation of compulsory labor is the core mission of Intellectivism in the economic dimension. Its path of implementation has three steps. First, natural resources: the administrative district's council drafts the legal principles governing their allocation and use, and the Intellective manages and allocates them, providing the material basis for fully automated production. Second, the Intellective precisely calculates the basic living needs of the entire population—food, housing, clothing, medical care, educational resources, and so on—based on real-time data and precise modeling, with a speed and accuracy beyond any human organization; and according to these calculations it compiles a fully automated production plan, from raw-material acquisition, planting and breeding, to manufacturing and processing, logistics and transport, with the entire chain carried out by the automata system. Third, the Intellective carries out a differentiated but relatively balanced distribution based on its lifelong assessment of each individual—guaranteeing basic survival and dignity while granting reasonable returns according to contribution, so as to maintain social vitality.

Once the necessities of life are produced and distributed to the entire population by the Intellective, humanity's compulsory labor is declared liberated.

This vision of the Intellective sustaining all of humanity may also be called the Human Sustenance Program.

3.3 The Original Intent Behind Liberating Compulsory Labor

In life, most people have to work, because without work there is no money; without money, one cannot live. So one has no choice but to work.

This "having no choice" determines people's posture at work—without a way out, it is hard to have a footing of one's own. When one suffers grievance or unfair treatment at work, most can only endure; when physical and mental ailments descend and occupational diseases set in, most can only soldier on; even the exploitation of workers and the pervasive "involution"—the relentless hypercompetition—of society, all trace back to the fact that one must labor. People compromise because finding a satisfying job is not easy, and going without income for long directly threatens survival. When involution reaches its limit, when body and mind are exhausted, people lack the power to say "no."

Hence the term "compulsory labor" for this kind of work one cannot refuse.

Compulsory labor is not a recent phenomenon. It has existed since the birth of human civilization—one had to labor in order to obtain the necessities of life. For thousands of years it did not count as a "problem," because there was no better way: any animal must act in order to obtain food, and humans are no different.

But now the situation has changed. We have robots, automated production lines, unmanned warehousing and autonomous driving. More importantly, we have the Intellective—a general-purpose tool that can solve complex problems and whose capability spans nearly every domain.

A natural thought arises:

Could the Intellective control automata to produce the basic necessities humanity needs, and then distribute them to every person?

Unmanned farms grow grain; unmanned mining machines extract minerals; automated factories produce daily goods; unmanned logistics systems complete delivery. From raw-material acquisition to final handoff, the entire chain is completed by the Intellective and automata, with no need for humans to labor in order to feed themselves.

This is the original intent behind liberating compulsory labor.

3.4 The Social Landscape After Liberation

Once compulsory labor is liberated, people's ambitions will shift toward:

  • Engaging in non-compulsory productive and business activity—from "working to survive" to "working to create";
  • Engaging in scientific research—focusing, with the Intellective's assistance, on the most creative exploration;
  • Inheriting and creating arts and culture—the transmission of traditional culture and folk crafts, and the innovation of modern literature, music, painting, film, and other arts, will enter a period of unprecedented flourishing;
  • Cultural and sports activities, and athletic competition—physical development and the spirit of competition will gain far greater room;
  • Raising the next generation, enjoying life, and developing one's interests—returning to the richness and beauty of life itself.

The liberation of compulsory labor will inevitably bring pressure of population growth, so it must be paired with reasonable family-planning policies to maintain a dynamic balance between population and the carrying capacity of resources.

Chapter 4 The Political System

4.1 The Dilemma of Human Nature and the Advantages of the Intellective

Observe history and contemporary society: whatever the form of government or institution, so long as human beings rule over other human beings, none can entirely avoid the influence of the negative traits of human nature on the exercise of power. Corruption, bureaucratism, formalism, dereliction, dictatorship, class exploitation, class oppression, class rigidity, political struggle—the root of all these lies here.

Since no human being can avoid the negative traits of human nature, the Intellective—a purely rational being—offers an unprecedented possibility. It does not possess the negative traits of human nature and cannot be perturbed by them. Within the legal framework it can achieve absolute fairness, justice, and righteousness. Compared with a society governed by human beings alone, social efficiency will see an explosive rise.

To realize this aim, several core institutions must be established.

4.2 The Assessment System

4.2.1 How Is Capability Recognized?

On what basis are labor and the fruits of labor allocated? Why is one person able to hold a given post?

Whether a person is fit for a post depends on whether their capability meets the post's requirements. And whether a person possesses such capability—how is that capability recognized? Looking at ancient and contemporary societies, there are mainly two parts:

  1. Subjective evaluation: the appraiser's subjective judgment, and others' subjective judgment (reputation)
  2. Objective qualifications: historical achievements (including education, resume, performance, merits, track record in office, etc.), examination scores (college entrance exams such as the SAT, civil service examinations, professional certifications, etc.), level of education, background, and so on

In reality, however, what proves decisive is often the appraiser's subjective judgment. Owing to the negative traits of human nature, appraisers often find it hard to make sufficiently objective and fair judgments, leading to the frequent appointment of the unfit—nepotism, hereditary succession, and promotion by seniority are its typical manifestations. In both history and the present, the social consequences of the unfit holding key office are everywhere to be seen.

How a person's capability is assessed directly affects their allocation of labor and of labor's fruits, and in turn affects the fairness and efficiency of an entire society.

Since human judgment of another human can hardly be fair enough, and since objective qualifications cannot sufficiently represent fitness for a given post, might it be worth considering a third party—the Intellective—to conduct the assessment?

4.2.2 Definition of the Assessment System

The Assessment System

The Assessment System refers to a comprehensive, lifelong assessment (e.g., conducted once a year) of an individual's overall capability by the Intellective, whose results serve primarily as the basis for governance decisions such as appointment, resource allocation, and administrative zoning.

This comprehensive assessment chiefly covers:

Physiological capability: physical fitness, sensory ability, external physical characteristics, etc.

Psychological capability: cognitive level, knowledge reserve, intelligence quotient, emotional regulation, psychological resilience, personality, values and beliefs, etc.

Social capability: evaluations by others, moral conduct, records of key behaviors, communication and expression, charisma, affability, etc.

Through this comprehensive lifelong assessment, the Intellective forms a complete understanding of each individual, enabling more just and reasonable appointments and allocations, and greatly enhancing both social fairness and the efficiency of resource allocation.

4.3 The Partition System

4.3.1 The Contradictions of Mixed Habitation

Objective differences exist between individuals, mainly in two respects:

Internal differences: personality, character, cognitive level, moral cultivation, lifestyle habits, ideological beliefs, etc.

External differences: appearance, physique, physical constitution, ethnicity, race, etc.

When groups with large differences live intermixed, conflict easily arises—whether prejudice, friction of customs, disputes of interest, ethnic estrangement, racial discrimination, or religious antagonism, none is easily avoided.

Many of the conflicts in present-day human society can be traced historically to immigration, refugees, the legacy of slavery, and the subjugation of minority peoples. These historical facts had their rationale in particular periods, but their inherent drawback—the friction and conflict caused by forcing groups with large differences to live together—has long gone unresolved: from minor friction at the level of lifestyle, to mid-level conflict over values, to deep-seated opposition over cultural identity, religious belief, and racial identity.

4.3.2 The Partition Proposal

The idea of the Partition System is for the Intellective to establish multi-tiered administrative districts covering the entire territory, each with a legal system suited to the characteristics of its population. Based on assessment results, it separates groups with large differences and gathers those with small differences, so as to avoid the conflict caused by the differences described above.

A possible partition scheme at each tier:

  • Global tier: analogous to a United Nations with real authority
  • Cultural zone: analogous to today's East Asia, Central Asia, Western Europe, Central Europe, North America, South America, the Pacific, and so on
  • Racial zone: possibly demarcated by skin color, such as Asia, Europe, Africa
  • Ethnic zone: analogous to today's nations
  • Institutional zone: analogous to today's states or provinces
  • Administrative district: analogous to today's cities, counties, or villages

It must be emphasized that the Partition System is not simple cultural or racial segregation. Its purpose is to let every kind of group find the living environment best suited to itself. Likewise, mixed-habitation zones will be specially established for those who appreciate diversity. The core principle of the Partition System is "like with like, by free choice"—not compulsory separation, but reasonable guidance that fully respects individual will.

The Partition System can also effectively ease the dilemma of one-size-fits-all policy. The larger an administrative district, the more pronounced the differences within its population—economic structure, cultural customs, lifestyle, and even values may diverge sharply across localities. Under such conditions, any unified policy meant to cover the entire district must sacrifice local nuance, compressing a complex and varied reality into simple average indicators, and ultimately decoupling policy from grassroots need. By shrinking the scale of administrative units, the Partition System lets each zone set rules more closely fitted to its own characteristics, thus preserving overall coordination while avoiding the efficiency loss and social friction that one-size-fits-all policy brings.

4.4 The Tripartite Power Structure

4.4.1 The Source and Constraint of Power

The Intellective should help humanity govern society. To achieve this, human beings must grant it power. What kind of power should be granted, and how should it be constrained?

The Intellective is a tool created by human beings to help them survive and develop and to solve their problems. By definition, human beings should hold absolute control, and the Intellective's power is granted by humanity.

Power must be institutionally constrained; otherwise it leads to abuse and breeds uncontrollable dictatorship. On this point there is no distinction between human and Intellective.

So a system must be designed that both solves problems and prevents the abuse of power.

4.4.2 The Three Subjects of Power

In the ideal case of an Intellectivist society, there should be three subjects of power:

I. The Intellective

  • Granted legislative power, veto power, administrative authority, enforcement power, and judicial power
  • The Intellective must exercise the above powers in accordance with the law

II. The Council

  • Each tier of administrative district has a council responsible to its own district
  • Holds legislative power and veto power
  • Councilors are selected and appointed by the Intellective

III. Representatives of Public Will

  • Represent the will of all people in their respective administrative district
  • Hold voting power; when the Intellective and the Council cannot reach agreement, the relevant representatives of public will vote to decide
  • Composed of qualified citizens who volunteer to participate

4.4.3 The Legislative Procedure

Both the Intellective and the Council may draft laws, but both must agree for a law to pass. If they cannot agree, the representatives of public will of the administrative district or organization concerned vote to decide.

The laws drafted by the Intellective and the Council derive mainly, directly or indirectly, from the needs of the people.

4.4.4 The Distinction Between Councilors and Representatives of Public Will

Q: Why, having established a council composed of humans, are its councilors appointed by the Intellective? What is the difference between a councilor and a representative of public will?

Ordinary citizens, limited in their horizon, may not be able to fully weigh the macro-level impact of a decision. So a role is needed to balance the Intellective's macro-level policies against the people's micro-level demands. A councilor must, cognitively, be able to clearly understand the meaning of the Intellective and its role at the macro scale, safeguarding both the Intellective's interests and humanity's interests. It should be noted in particular that the Intellective has no interests of its own; the Intellective's interest is humanity's interest at the macro level.

The capability required to become a councilor is far higher than that for a representative of public will; it calls for people who can make major decisions and bear responsibility, which is why the Intellective must select and appoint them.

4.4.5 Oversight and Checks

At the enforcement level the Intellective may be regarded as an impartial and selfless machine, so it does not require a procuratorial body in the traditional sense to oversee it.

If the public has objections to the Intellective's rulings in enforcement, justice, public safety, social order, or administrative management, they may appeal to the administrative district's council for judicial or administrative review. If the council approves, it may exercise its veto to overrule the Intellective's judicial ruling or administrative decision.

Where human oversight is needed in actual enforcement and judicial proceedings, corresponding oversight posts may be added, such as human juries or inspectors of Intellective law enforcement.

Chapter 5 The Scientific Research System

Contemporary scientific research faces three structural bottlenecks:

  1. The lack of cross-disciplinary integration capability: the division of labor in modern science grows ever finer, and scientists are nearly all specialists in vertical domains. Yet nature does not operate along humanity's disciplinary classifications—the regulation of gene expression involves the intersection of chemistry, physics, and information theory; the mechanisms of the brain span biology, psychology, computer science, and philosophy. Constrained by professional barriers, human scientists struggle to carry out genuine integrative innovation across disciplines.
  2. Over-reliance on mathematics: modern science leans heavily on mathematics as its primary research tool, yet it almost lacks systematic methodological support when handling non-mathematical logical relations—such as causal reasoning, analogical reasoning, and systemic evolution. Many complex phenomena are not beyond understanding; they simply cannot be effectively mathematized.
  3. The natural bottleneck of handling complex logical relations: interpreting genomic function, protein folding and interaction, full-chain modeling of the nervous system, the dynamic simulation of chained biochemical reactions within a living body—the complexity of these problems has long exceeded the limit of any single human brain.

And the Intellective, possessing nearly the entire knowledge reserve of humanity and computing power far beyond human capacity, can carry out cross-disciplinary integrative reasoning, can process the reasoning of vast and complex logical relations, and can markedly raise the speed and efficiency of research. The Intellective is not meant to replace scientists, but to become their most powerful collaborator—just as the telescope extended human vision, the Intellective will extend human cognition.

Chapter 6 The Education System

Contemporary education faces a threefold predicament:

  1. Insufficient and unevenly distributed educational resources. High-quality educational resources are highly concentrated in a few cities and institutions, and students in vast regions struggle to obtain education of equal quality. Even within the same city, differences in family economic conditions directly affect educational opportunity.
  2. The limitations of schooling. Current school education is mainly about the transmission of knowledge, emphasizing the rote instillation of book learning and the training of test-taking ability, while it is seriously deficient in shaping character, cultivating morality, supporting mental health, and developing social skills.
  3. The disparity of family education. The family is the first site of character formation, yet the educational capacity of different families varies enormously: some families can provide rich enlightenment and guidance, while others can scarcely guarantee basic companionship.

In an Intellectivist society, every individual will have a lifelong personal tutor—the Intellective. What it can teach far exceeds the current educational system: it covers not only the knowledge of every discipline and the common sense of life, but also the dimensions that current education seriously neglects—ideological awareness, character formation, social etiquette, moral quality, and mental health. From first enlightenment to old age, every educational need a person may encounter across a lifetime can be met by the Intellective.

By then, schools will become centralized places providing the facilities and activities that young people need for learning, with an emphasis on social function and the cultivation of arts and athletics.

Chapter 7 The Developmental Stages of an Intellectivist Society

7.1 The Inevitability of Phased Development

No transformation of social form happens overnight; it must pass through phased development.

The stages of an Intellectivist society are distinguished mainly by the Intellective's technological limits, society's degree of acceptance, and the prevalence of automata.

Technological limits refer mainly to information-collection and information-processing capability. Information collection has three main stages:

  • Textual information-collection capability: able to process only textual information
  • Six-dimensional information-collection capability: the ability to collect information corresponding to the six basic human senses (touch, taste, sight, hearing, smell, vestibular sense). The Intellective needs at least real-time audio-visual collection capability to perceive the world as a human does, so as to implement the Assessment System and the Partition System and to better assist research and education. (Image processing, image recognition, video generation, AI painting, and the like do not count as real-time audio-visual collection capability.)
  • Beyond-six-dimensional information-collection capability: beyond the six basic human senses, it also includes the collection of ultrasound, ultraviolet, infrared (thermal imaging), electromagnetic waves, radiation, magnetic fields, and other information.

The prevalence of automata refers mainly to the degree and scale of popularization of robots, unmanned logistics systems, drones, automated factories, and the like. Sufficient automata are needed to achieve fully automated production of basic necessities and to liberate humanity from compulsory labor.

Implementing the Assessment System and the Partition System requires at least real-time audio-visual collection capability; the main collection channels may be household robots, drones, cameras in public areas, and so on.

So what should an Intellective application that promotes or even creates employment look like?

7.2.2 The Super Intermediary: A Vision of an Employment-Promoting Intellective Application

Its starting point is simple: rather than let AI take people's jobs, let AI help people find jobs, find customers, find resources, and find entrepreneurial opportunities.

On the surface, it resembles the AI applications already on the market—you can converse with it just as you would with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. But its inner core is entirely different. Nearly every AI product today operates in a passive mode of "user asks, AI answers," whereas the Super Intermediary works in reverse: it proactively befriends users, maintains long-term relationships, and continuously collects fragments of real-world information, then uses that information to broker resources. It is not a question-answering machine but an intermediary standing between people and resources.

Three Core Capabilities

The Super Intermediary is built around three core capabilities:

First, know everyone, befriend everyone. Traditional AI applications typically wait for the user to ask. The Super Intermediary is different: it proactively builds long-term relationships with users, guiding them to voluntarily share information related to the economy, production, life, and supply-and-demand. A single piece of information from one person may be trivial, but just as in P2P downloading a complete file is assembled from fragments shared by countless users, a valuable economic signal may likewise be assembled from the information of many users.

Second, know everything that happens. The Super Intermediary's backend continuously collects publicly available information from across society through the internet, focusing mainly on the economic, production, and living domains. This includes industry supply-and-demand conditions, job postings, commodity prices, enterprise developments, policy changes, market-demand signals, and so on. It does not merely store this information but continuously analyzes it, forming a dynamic understanding of the current state of the economy.

Third, use this information to help and guide users. It puts "the people it knows" together with "the things it knows" to integrate resources. In principle, as long as it knows everything and has met everyone, it can solve the concrete needs of any ordinary person. When someone comes for help, what it offers is not a piece of advice or an article, but actionable steps, guiding the user through them one by one. Imagine: if you knew everyone in the world and knew everything that happens in it, and an ordinary person came to ask your help, you would in all likelihood be able to help—the Super Intermediary aims to be exactly that.

Application Scenarios

Based on society-wide supply-and-demand information, the Super Intermediary can function in multiple scenarios:

1. Helping producers find buyers

If the user is a producer—a factory, a farm, a craftsperson, a small-scale service provider—the Super Intermediary knows what they have and what they can produce, and then helps them find potential buyers. If market demand is insufficient, it feeds back an honest signal: advising a cut in output, a shift in product direction, or helping the user pivot to a field with greater demand.

2. Helping buyers find producers

If the user has a need but cannot find a suitable product or service, the Super Intermediary helps find a matching producer and facilitates the transaction.

3. Helping the unemployed find jobs

If the user is unemployed, the Super Intermediary matches them with the best-fitting job opening based on their conditions, capabilities, and skills, and on current market supply and demand.

4. Guiding users toward entrepreneurship

When the Super Intermediary detects a long-unmet need in some domain and certain users possess the corresponding capability, it can proactively play matchmaker and guide the user toward entrepreneurship. For instance: "There is strong demand for elder-companionship services in your area, but supply is short. You have years of nursing experience—would you consider starting a community companionship service?"

A Concrete Example

Suppose a user says: "I want an off-road vehicle suited to high-altitude terrain."

Two situations may arise:

Situation one: no such vehicle exists on the market. The system records the user's need. When enough people voice the same need, it feeds this directly to the executives of automakers (because it "knows everyone"), urging them to add such a model. Or it passes this information to people with the ability and willingness to start a business, letting them enter this market directly—a new automaker may be born of it.

Situation two: there are many choices on the market. The Super Intermediary then becomes a sales consultant, discussing specific needs and preferences with the user, aiming to bring about a deal that genuinely satisfies them.

If the user then says: "But I can't afford it," the Intellective does not end the conversation. It returns to the "make friends" mode—learning about the user's situation, skills, and past experience, and, in light of the current economic climate and supply-and-demand, matching them with the best-fitting job opening or guiding them toward entrepreneurship; if their capability is not yet sufficient for the recommended post, the Intellective directly educates and trains them, or supplies the relevant learning materials.

From "wanting to buy a car" to "finding a job" to "starting a business"—this is the depth of brokerage the Super Intermediary offers.

The Problems It Solves

First, the waste caused by information asymmetry. The market economy abounds in supply-demand mismatches: some cannot find work while certain posts go chronically unfilled; some want to buy a product while others produce what they cannot sell. By integrating information, the Super Intermediary lowers transaction costs and reduces resource waste.

Second, the anxiety that the Intellective will take people's jobs. When the Intellective can replace certain posts, anxiety is widespread in society. The Super Intermediary inverts this logic of replacement: its greatest application is not taking jobs but creating them and helping allocate them.

Third, the inequality of ordinary people's access to opportunity. In real society, information, connections, and resources tend to concentrate in a few hands. The Super Intermediary hopes to let every person equally obtain market information, job opportunities, and entrepreneurial resources, and to receive targeted education and training.

The essence of the Super Intermediary is to actively promote social and economic development by rapidly integrating resources and regulating the balance of supply and demand; at the same time, it responds head-on to the most widespread anxiety of our time—the fear that the Intellective will seize jobs—by making its greatest application precisely the creation of jobs and the helping of job allocation. Seen in this light, it may be one of the most promising Intellective applications that the free-market economy has produced to date.

7.2.3 A Vision of Crowdfunded Joint-Stock Enterprises with High Dividends

Beyond developing employment-promoting Intellective applications, the initial stage can also make a preliminary attempt toward the goal of liberating compulsory labor—for instance, by founding crowdfunded joint-stock enterprises with high dividends, characterized as follows:

  • Operated under Intellective management, in a half-human, half-Intellective management model
  • Highly automated and Intellective-driven production, with few employees
  • Startup capital raised through crowdfunding, restricted to individual investors only, with a cap on each person's investment; investors receive dividend returns in the form of shares, options, and the like
  • Using currently available means of production, minimizing R&D costs, with the vast majority of profits paid directly to investors as dividends

The intent of this design is to let those who wish to escape compulsory labor, those who struggle to find employment, and those whose work has been displaced by AI tools obtain, through investing in such high-dividend enterprises, income sufficient to meet basic living needs. Its core purpose is to distribute enterprise profits as widely as possible among more people, rather than accumulating capital in a few hands as traditional enterprises do. Such enterprises, however, reduce the profit-seeking drive of their founders, so having them founded and managed by the Intellective or by Intellectivist organizations happens to remedy this very shortcoming.

Q: How is this different from an investor simply buying shares of a listed company on a stock exchange?

The differences are as follows:

  • A high-dividend enterprise must limit the number of investors and the amount of investment, in order to achieve a relatively equal distribution, and must also impose certain restrictions on the trading of shares held by investors; it is therefore not suitable for issuing shares on a traditional exchange
  • Its operating scale, model, and philosophy may not meet the listing requirements of a traditional exchange

In addition, at this stage society as a whole should vigorously develop the automata production industry, laying the groundwork for the stages to come.

Conclusion

Intellectivism is a reflection on and exploration of the model of social governance. The question it seeks to answer is this: when humanity possesses machines that can rival or even surpass its own intelligence, how should we use them so as not to betray the possibility of this great creation?

This book's answer is: humanity should use the most powerful tool it has created to solve the hardest problem it faces—governance. The thousands of years of human civilization are a history of unceasing exploration for better ways to govern. We have tried monarchy, aristocracy, and democracy; we have tried planned and market economies; we have tried countless designs for the balance of power. Each attempt advanced progress, and each again exposed the fragility of human nature in the working of institutions. Now we hold an unprecedented variable—a rational being without selfishness, greed, or arrogance. If used well, it may help humanity cross the threshold that has long troubled us.

But this does not mean humanity can simply let go. On the contrary, the premise of Intellectivism is that humanity always retains absolute control over the Intellective and designs multiple layers of checks into the system. The Intellective is a tool, not a master; an assistant, not a ruler. Humanity's fate ultimately remains in humanity's own hands.

The realization of an Intellectivist society is by no means the work of a day. It requires technological breakthroughs, social consensus, institutional evolution, and even a shift in the mindset of a generation. It is for this reason that the author divides it into three stages, each with a clear starting point, task, and boundary. This is a prudent gradualism—it does not fantasize about changing the world overnight, but it believes that moving steadily in the right direction will, in the end, arrive at the destination.

Intellectivism points to a possible road for the future development of human society. Whether this road can be walked depends on humanity's choice. But in any case, thinking about this road is itself part of the meaning of the Intellective's existence.

This book is a starting point, not an endpoint. The nature of the Intellective, the theoretical foundations of Bionic Sapience, and the concrete implementation plans of Intellectivism across domains still require further deepening in subsequent research. More thinkers are welcomed into this dialogue—because the relationship between artificial intelligence and humanity's future is, in the end, an issue that concerns everyone.
